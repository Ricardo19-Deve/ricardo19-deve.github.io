ENGINEERING CASE STUDY / 06

Salesforce Signal Room.

From sales data to weekly commitments, inside Salesforce.

Open-source module · Apex · Lightning Web Components · Experience Cloud
THE CONTEXT

What I set out to solve.

Field sales teams sit on months of sales data, yet the signals in it are found late or not at all: a hospital that slowly moves one component to a competitor, a surgeon who starts operating somewhere else, a tender deadline that slips past, a contract that is quietly under-delivered. When analytics exist, they usually live outside the CRM, so nothing connects them to the pipeline or to a weekly follow-up.

This module keeps the whole loop on the platform the team already works in, published as a neutral open-source reference with fictitious data.

THE ENGINEERING QUESTION

Explainable before intelligent.

How can opportunities be found automatically in sales data, explained with the numbers behind them so a rep can challenge them, and followed up every week until they become committed actions, without an external analytics stack and without letting AI decide what is true?

LAYER 01 / 04

Data

Monthly units and revenue per account and product line, account profiles (segment, procedure volumes, rollout phase), surgeons and the hospitals they operate in, public tenders read from the EU and UK procurement APIs, volume contracts and hospital volumes from a public registry. Demo data is generated with planted patterns so every rule has something to find.

IDENTIFY

Rules that show their working.

Fifteen rules in eleven types look for churn, partial switches to a competitor, missing components, robotic workflows, white space against similar hospitals, surgeons who use the product elsewhere, new surgeons, tenders closing soon, low share of wallet and contracts that run under or over their commitment.

Each pointer stores the facts that triggered it and a monthly chart, so the rationale is a set of numbers, not a score from a black box. Re-running the engine updates the numbers but never overwrites a rep's decision, and pointers that are no longer detected expire.

ACTIVATE

From pointer to Opportunity.

Reps review the pointers ranked by value and urgency, accept them, reject them with a reason or turn them into an Opportunity in the org's first open stage. The acceptance rate per rule shows which rules need tuning.

An optional language model, reached only through a Named Credential, writes the why, the what, next best actions, talking points and discovery questions. Without it, every screen works with a rule-based summary.

EXECUTE

The same 7 questions every 7 days.

A weekly Signal Room session per team reviews wins and losses, new pointers, accepted pointers without action, overdue commitments, stuck opportunities and tenders closing soon, and records what the team commits to for next week, with owners and due dates. An optional briefing prepares the facilitator.

CONTROL

Contracts against delivered volume.

Volume contracts hold a committed quantity per month and product line. The portal compares committed and delivered units month by month, with the cumulative variance, the six-month run-rate and a projection to the end of the term, and rates each line On track, Under or Over against a tolerance.

A shortfall becomes a win-back pointer, an overrun a prompt to renegotiate the tier, and a contract that ends within six months a renewal pointer.

MARKET

Public registry volumes per hospital.

A market view imports the public CSV export of the UK National Joint Registry. The browser parses the file and keeps the last 24 complete months, so only aggregates reach Salesforce, with no scraping and no callout. Hospitals are matched to accounts by hospital or trust name, and linked accounts use the real procedure volumes for share of wallet and peer sizing.

DESIGN SYSTEM

Neutral in every org.

Custom dropdowns, switches and buttons replace themed base components, so the portal looks the same whatever the brand colour of the org, in a Lightning tab and in a full-screen Experience Cloud (LWR) site. The line charts use a colour palette checked for colour-blind separation, with a legend, a crosshair tooltip and a table view.

TENDERS

Real public tenders, matched to accounts.

A batch reads TED (EU), Find a Tender (UK) and Public Contracts Scotland through their public APIs, one item per source or 30-day window so every portal gets its own limits, with all callouts before any write. Notices are kept when a lot uses one of the configured CPV codes, normalised into one shape (earliest open deadline, lots summed, value converted to EUR, link to the official notice), categorised from keywords in several languages and de-duplicated across portals.

Buyers are matched to accounts of the same country after removing accents and noise words in several languages; each part of a long buyer name is scored, and a link is only automatic when two words agree. Linked open tenders become tender radar pointers, and human statuses and links survive every sync.

GUIDE

Every number, explained in the portal.

A guide view explains each page: what it shows, where the data comes from and how every number is calculated, from the priority score to contract projections and tender matching. It reads the live rule settings of the org, so the documentation cannot drift from what the engine uses, and it doubles as a ten-minute demo script. Every page links to its own section.

ADOPTION

A tour on every page.

A Tour button next to each page title dims the screen and highlights every block of the page, about a hundred steps across eight pages: what each block is for, how its numbers are calculated and where the data comes from. The tour opens what it needs by itself (an account, a session, a contract, a pointer, an admin tab), waits for the data to load and skips blocks that have no data; a script walks every step to prove none is missing. The same step texts feed the guide, which documents each page block by block with setup, roles, routines, the data model and troubleshooting, so the tour and the documentation cannot drift apart.

TEAMS AND RULES

Manager view and rules without code.

All, Mine and My team scopes follow the standard manager field of the user, so a sales manager sees the work of their reps. The demo adds six fictitious reps as free Chatter users, created with the welcome e-mail switched off. Rules are edited in a dialog that only shows the parameters each rule type uses, and admins can add a new rule of any of the eleven types or delete the ones they added.

ENGINEERING PRACTICES

Built to be handed over.

Apex tests that run as a user with only the module permission set and plant every pattern the rules look for, Apex tests for the tender feeds with mocked portal responses, Jest tests for the shared helpers, the registry CSV parser, the tender view, the guide and the page tours, linting and tests in continuous integration, header comments for future maintainers, a demo data generator with a compact mode for the 5 MB of a Developer Edition org, and documentation of the rules, the data model and a talk track, plus the in-portal guide.
