# M V53 — professional visitor guide + continuous tail motion

## Why

The public portfolio is meant to introduce Ricardo Pinheiro, demonstrate
his professional story, technical work and products, and help visitors find
his public contact links. Previously Ask M used the character's fictional
"small lights" narration even when asked "O que podes dizer sobre o Ricardo?",
making career answers sound disconnected from the professional purpose.

## Professional answer architecture

- `m-ai/professional-guide.mjs` now distinguishes business and career
  questions from questions about **M's own fictional identity**.
- Professional requests use a **separate system prompt**, curated facts and
  visitor-facing style rules — not the fictional encounter narrative.
- Professional answers begin with Ricardo, his experience or the capability
  requested. They explain his Salesforce/enterprise-systems work, integrations,
  development and independent software products with a concise narrative.
- A practical, non-pushy contact path points to the existing Contact section
  and its public LinkedIn and GitHub profile links.
- The curated professional story and `waysToHelp` are grounded in
  `m-ai/portfolio-context.mjs`, itself reviewed from public `index.html`.
- **Do not invent commercial availability, prices, service packages,
  client results, private employer details, contact emails or achievements.**
  The question "Que serviços presta?" gets verifiable capabilities phrased
  as "pode ajudar em", not unsupported sales promises.
- Non-LinkedIn questions do **not** receive the unavailable LinkedIn
  snapshot as distracting context.
- If an AI output nevertheless starts with a fictional lights/recantos
  metaphor on a Ricardo profile/service/contact question, a conservative
  server-side fallback replaces it with a public-facts-only answer.
  Navigation-only requests remain untouched and the allowed section
  suggestions continue to be governed by the original navigation policy.
- The playful personality, original encounters and fictional little-light
  adventure remain available **only for questions about M**.

## Motion V53

- The eleven autonomous mini-performances from V51 are intact.
- The tail now has a **continuous second-order critically damped follow**
  per original tail joint, with gradually slower response towards the tip.
- Follow targets depend on the real attention vector and actual turning
  signals already generated by the portfolio world — no new tracking or
  network calls.
- Bone rotations and angular velocities continue through the V41 pose
  handoff, with original V28 expression continuity.
- Sleep, rest, gestures, fast movement, reading, verified card attachment
  and `prefers-reduced-motion` remain authoritative: the play layer does
  not interrupt them. The authored envelope reaches zero at the exact
  conclusion of a miniature, preserving the original V6 rest pose.
- `?v=53` revision is consistent across the portfolio entry, stylesheet,
  main runtime imports and existing cache-parity tests.

## Safety and scope

- **LinkedIn integration continues to read ONLY the owner's publicly shared
  posts**, via `MEMBER_SHARE_INFO` and strict PUBLIC/EVERYONE validation.
- No LinkedIn messages, contacts, connections, profile data or other account
  domains are accessed for the motion or the professional-answer changes.
- The LinkedIn OAuth token remains a Cloudflare Secret; no new credential
  or permission is required.
- The M model, page layout, routes and live service configuration are
  preserved.

## Validation

New `m-ai/professional-guide.test.mjs` tests professional-versus-character
question classification, professional prompt isolation, original section
navigation, the sample whimsical lead fallback, contact answers and privacy.

New `m-world-v53-tail-flow.test.mjs` tests no abrupt tail reset, joint
momentum, exact zero at authored action endpoints, genuine interrupt
handling, and no browser/LinkedIn access from animation.

The GitHub Pages and Worker publication jobs are separate. Node tests do
not substitute for a real visual WebGL review; long-standing legacy
Chrome checks for old chat controls and desktop text-shield pixels may
still report unrelated failures.
